## 1. The pitch

Twelve years ago, the **GAMERS** won the **Great Yiff War** and sealed the **FURRIES** under **Mt. Dew** — a mountain made of gamer trash — behind a Barrier of closed-bedroom stench and expired body spray. Then they closed the curtains and never came out. Twelve years of winning turned a whole race into **NEETs**; up there they call it *goblin mode*. Down below, the furries think that's literal.

**Grub**, 26, a jaded gamer NEET who lives in his mom's basement wrapped in a blanket, only leaves the house when the Mountain Dew runs out. On the way back from the store, **30 minutes before his squad's Fortnite match**, he cuts across Mt. Dew to get home faster, slips on a **Tide Pod** and falls into the Rift. The clock is the urgency of the whole game.

Down there, every furry wants the same thing: **for him to say "yes."** A gamer soul can only be converted **willingly** — and the King needs one more to break the Barrier and **furrify the entire surface**. That's why the Underground is so nice. The hugs, the glitter Pop-Tarts, the blankets, the "you're safe here": it's all **love-bombing**.

**The game in one sentence:** Undertale, except the danger isn't dying — it's **liking it**.

- **Main threat:** furrification. Every hit makes Grub fluffier (the **FUR** meter). HP 0 = **WELCOME HOME**.
- **Alternate route:** if FUR reaches 100% and Grub **accepts**, he becomes a furry, his health bar **flips** (it becomes **FLUFF**) and the game gains an extra act: **his Fortnite squad drops into the Underground from the Battle Bus** — and Grub fights the gamers.
- **Butt of the joke:** furries are the main object of disgust; gamers are a close second, and just as gross.

## 2. Content and tone rules

### 2.1 What's allowed

| Allowed | How |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Sexual innuendo and gross-out humor** | Through **vocabulary and open reference**: fandom slang ("yiff", "OwO", "musk", "NSFW", "commissions", "horny jail"), hentai/ecchi on the gamer side, tabs closed in a hurry, suspicious folders, implication. It carries narrative weight: it *is* the satire. |
| **Furries as the main target** | Stinky fursuits, the zipper, musk, unsolicited hugs, "18+" art, commissions, zero shame, love-bombing. |
| **Gamers are gross too** | Hygiene, the bottle that isn't Mountain Dew, the "Homework" folder, the "ergonomic" mousepad, swimsuit gacha, Discord simps, the body pillow "washed once", the "she's actually a 1,000,000-year-old dragon" excuse. |
| **Real brands, by name and in the art** (v3) | The game is non-commercial, so brands show up **openly**, in text **and** drawn into the art with their real names: Doritos, Mountain Dew (Code Red, Baja Blast), Monster Energy, G FUEL, Axe, Febreze, Cheetos, Pop-Tarts, Tide, Discord, Steam. Small sprites carry the brand as **its packaging color plus one word** in pixel font (a green can with `DEW`, a red triangle bag with `DORITOS`). **Adult brands** (e.g. **Bad Dragon**) appear only as **a logo or a name on a closed object**: a sealed package, a banner over a curtain. Nothing about the product is shown. There is **no brand swap** for the public build. |
| **Floofy's friendship petals** (v3.2, user decision D-20) | The **one exception** to "adult products stay closed": Floofy's petals are **flower-shaped adult toys** (the rose-shaped one first; a small capsule "bullet" for the pun). They are drawn **only as bullets**: tiny white-on-black pixel objects (8–16 px) that read as a rose or a capsule, with a 2-frame buzz wobble. **v3.5:** one exception to the size, the **giant petal** of the R2 fake tutorial (§19.0): the same rose-shaped toy drawn as big as the battle box, white on black, still a closed object with the buzz wobble, read as "a rose that is clearly not a rose". **Never in use, never touching or next to a body, no anatomy, no brand, no packaging.** The joke is the vocabulary (`good vibes`, `filled with them`), the buzzing and the narrator refusing (`* The petals are vibrating.` / `* (Why are they vibrating.)`). They appear only in Floofy's fights (R2, §19.0; the Mascot's `bouquet_mouth`, §19.7) and as the "rose" in his paw (§10.10). |
| **Other games, by name, look and sound** (v3) | Other games can be referenced openly: their item names, HUDs and iconic sounds (RuneScape, League of Legends, Counter-Strike, Call of Duty, Portal, Half-Life, Minecraft, Fortnite, StarCraft, Dark Souls, FNAF). Most of it is concentrated in the Toxic route's HUD (§8.9); see rule 7 in §2.3. |

### 2.2 What's not allowed (fixed rules)

| Forbidden | Detail |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Nothing visually explicit** | No nudity, acts or anatomy in sprites, backgrounds or cutscenes. The visual ceiling is **mosaic censorship** as a joke and suggestive objects that stay closed (tab, folder, curtain). |
| **Children** | No children in the game, no references to children, no character with a childlike look. Every character is an **adult**. Undertale's Monster Kid **has no parody**. Fandom vocabulary tied to minors (e.g. "cub") is **forbidden**, and so is coded slang for abuse material (e.g. "cheese pizza"), even as a "food". Meme foods that come from kids' habits (eating glue) get adult framing, never school. |
| **Hate-adjacent memes** (v3) | **Pepe the Frog and its variants** (Groyper, etc.) are not used anywhere: the meme was co-opted by hate groups. The protagonist is **Grub** (§10.1). Same rule for any meme mostly known for hate-group use. The name easter egg `PEPE` only answers `* Not that frog.` |
| **Controlled exception: the dragon excuse** | Only as satire of **the gamer making the excuse**. The character he refers to **never appears, is never described and has no sprite**. The joke is the narrator cutting the gamer off (§18.5). |
| **Real animals** | No reference to acts involving real animals. Every "animal" in the game is a person's fursona/fursuit. |
| **Real people** | No real person named, depicted or recognizable (this includes faces from photo memes, YouTubers, streamers, and brands built around one person). Real-world events, sites and conventions become fictional too (e.g. the convention is **FurNation Con '14**). **Brands and games are the exception** (§2.1). **Gamer Girl Bath Water** (v3.2, D-21) is allowed as **the product name only**: a sealed jar with a label. The person who sold it is never named, drawn, described or alluded to, and there is no "gamer girl" character. |

### 2.3 How to make the "gross" humor work

1. **The implication is funnier than the thing.** The narrator refuses: `* No.` · `* You decide not to look.` · `* The narrator has muted this description.`
2. **Cut it off mid-sentence.** `Okay but technically—` `* No.`
3. **Closed object.** A curtain, a tab, a folder, a padlocked book. Examining it twice changes the reaction; it never opens.
4. **Ratio:** ~70% of the gross-out jokes land on furries, ~30% on gamers. Gamers show up more on the surface side (squad messages, Grub's room, "The Drop" act).
5. **The protagonist is original** (§10.1), never a frog: the old protagonist was dropped under §2.2. He is a plain human gamer NEET (v3.3, D-16); goblins exist only in furry myth (§4.1).
6. **Write the meme natively.** The jokes are English internet culture — ratio, touch grass, skill issue, "no cap", "it's giving", "rent free", "down bad", Reply Guys, copypastas. Use the real cadence of the format (a Discord message reads like a Discord message, a streamer reads like a streamer) instead of explaining the joke.
7. **References to other games ambush the player; they never pile up** (v3).
   - **Zero before the first kill.** The `FIRST BLOOD` only lands because the game had no references until then.
   - **Replace, don't add.** A hitsplat takes the place of the damage number, the killfeed takes the place of the dust line. No new joke on top of what's there.
   - **UI and sound, not dialogue.** Characters don't quote memes; the interface does. No caption, no explanation.
   - **One game per system** on the Toxic HUD (§8.9), so they don't fight each other.
   - **Outside the Toxic HUD, a low dose:** one or two per area, as examinables or one-room ideas (the Apurrture booth, the night-shift office, the floor messages).
   - **Avoid** the most worn-out ones: "sus", "the cake is a lie" said out loud, "Half-Life 3 confirmed" said out loud.

### 2.4 Overall tone
- **Comedic horror** on the main route: the Underground is cute and cozy, and scary precisely because of that.
- **Affection under the gross-out:** every character gets a moment of dignity (Barky afraid of not being accepted, Papyrus wanting to belong, the King in mourning).
- **Density:** no room without at least one funny examinable object; roughly one laugh every ~20 seconds.
- **Rule of three** in signs and repetitions; **callbacks** planted in the Ruins and paid off in the Con Center (§30).

## 4. Cosmology

### 4.1 The Surface
The world above, home of the Gamers. After the war nobody posts fanart anymore: just headshot clips, patch-note complaints and gacha banners. Closed bedrooms, monitor glow, bottles on the floor. It is technically a victory. Nobody seems happy.

**Goblin mode (v3.3).** The gamers won, closed the curtains and went **goblin mode**, the slang kind: no showers, no daylight, Doritos at 3 AM, the same hoodie for a month. Twelve years later the surface is a race of **NEETs**. They are still plain humans, just pale, hunched and wrapped in blankets. It happened slowly and nobody up there noticed. **The gamers never mention it**: the intro is their propaganda (§9), and to them it's normal.

**The goblin myth.** The furries only ever heard the slang. Sealed underground with one bar of signal, they caught `goblin mode` in memes and in what the fallen said, and took it **literally**. The Hall of Posts (§16) tells it as history, and its mural draws the gamers as green, pointy-eared goblins: `* They used to be human.` / `* Then they won. Then they went goblin mode.` Every gamer who actually falls is a pale human in a hoodie or a blanket, and the furries are a little let down each time (Furiell, R2: `You're not even green. The murals lied~`). The joke lives in the gap: the furries' story about the gamers is as wrong as the gamers' story about the furries (§9). (Replaces the v3 Goblinization, where the gamers turned into goblins for real.)

### 4.2 Mt. Dew
A mountain made of **12 years of gamer trash**: compacted cans, Dorito dust in layers ("the nacho stratum"), broken headsets, cables and bottles that are **not** soda. At the peak, a giant flat-brim cap. Legend: "you get 5G at the top" (a lie; one bar, sometimes). Halfway up is **the Rift**, with a hand-written sign: `FREE HUGS ↓`.

### 4.3 The Underground
The furry kingdom under Mt. Dew. It is the **FurNation Con '14** convention center, which sank whole on the day of the Sealing, plus everything the furries built around it over 12 years. No Wi-Fi: only a local network (the **Undernet**) hosting **the Archive** — 12 years of fandom art, "most of it behind the 18+ button."

| Area | What it is | Satire target |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **The Ruins of Con '14** | The convention's original halls: banners, booths, the Art Show with its 18+ section behind a curtain. | Conventions, zippers, fursuit smell, the fandom "mom" |
| **Snowdinha** | A village under the industrial AC (60 °F for 12 years), with an RGB LED aurora. Small-town vibes. | Streaming, wanting to belong, small-town Discord |
| **Wetfall** | Damp caverns: 12 years of condensed fursuit sweat turned into a waterfall. Smells like "wet dog." | Musk, DMs, commissions, species gatekeeping |
| **Hotland Data Center** | The Archive's server room. 113 °F. | Influencers, moderation, the Archive itself |
| **The Con Center** | The main hall, turned into a castle. Beige, quiet, participation trophies. | The war, grief, tribalism |
| **The Barrier** | Behind the throne. | — |

### 4.4 The Barrier
A wall of **greenish-yellow fog**: the combined stench of seven gamers who hadn't showered in a week, "masked" with **expired spray deodorant**, with a spinning "no signal" icon in the middle. It is the **ROTEIRO's Barrier** ("expired deodorant and nonexistent Wi-Fi").

```
* The air here smells like a closed bedroom.
* Your eyes water. You feel... at home?
```

## 5. History and timeline

### 5.1 Two versions of history
The game tells the war **twice**, and both versions are biased:
- **The gamer version** (the intro, §9): the winners' propaganda. Furries as "degenerates" who tried to convert everyone.
- **The furry version** (Hall of Posts, §16): the furries only wanted to "share" (the "Free Hugs Campaign")… they just shared too much, unprompted, on every game forum, at 3 AM.

**Neither is right.** Both sides were gross; only one of them won.

## 6. The rules of the world

> These rules are the game's "physics." Every mechanic and every line of dialogue has to respect them.

### 6.1 The gamer soul
Every gamer has a red **SOUL** made of **DETERMINATION** (and a little rage). It is the most powerful thing in the Underground: only gamer souls can cross the Barrier, and only **seven** gamer souls can undo it (seven made it).

### 6.2 Conversion is voluntary
- A gamer soul **only comes out willingly**. Nobody can rip it out.
- That's why the whole Underground is so nice: the strategy is **love-bombing**. Hugs, food, blankets, compliments on a fursona you don't even have — until the gamer says "yes."
- **Furrification** is the process: every contact with the Underground's cuteness (the bullets) deposits "fluff" on the gamer (the **FUR** meter). At 100% FUR, the gamer is ready for the question.
- When the gamer accepts, the **red soul leaves the chest** and a **pink plush soul** with little ears takes its place. The gamer becomes a furry and **slowly forgets they were a gamer** (only flashes remain: "one more match…").
- HP 0 is a **forced** attempt: the soul refuses (DETERMINATION), and the result is a **WELCOME HOME** — you "go back" to your last save.

### 6.3 The six jars
In the castle basement, six mason jars hold the red souls of the gamers who said "yes," labeled in the King's handwriting:

### 6.4 Shame (SHAME)
- Shame is the gamers' weapon and the only thing that **kills** a furry: anyone shamed hard enough **turns to dust** (Undertale's "dust").
- In main-route combat, Grub's **FIGHT** *is* this: he mocks, points, films. The hit is shame. That's why killed enemies turn into plush dust.
- On the Furry route, the same shame is what hurts Grub (FLUFF damage).

### 6.5 Floofy (v3.1)
**Floofy** is the Underground's self-appointed welcome committee: a flamboyant, zesty, flower-themed furry and **the furry who most wants to turn you into one**. He pounces, he OwOs, he never stops recruiting.

- **Why he has no shame:** after Azzy turned to dust, **Alphys** (the royal scientist) asked for a volunteer for her DETERMINATION experiment. Floofy raised both paws (`for the recruits!!`). The DETERMINATION burned away every bit of shame he had. **None** left: no disgust, guilt, embarrassment or limits.
- He has DETERMINATION, so he remembers saves and resets.
- His motto: **"In this world, it's YIFF or be YIFFED."**
- He wants the **seven souls** to become the **Supreme Fursona** and furrify the entire world, no limits at all. (He is, quite literally, the furry with the fewest boundaries in existence.)
- **Where the humor is:** in how furry and how zesty he is, always pushing you to "join" (`*pounces on you* OwO what's this?`). He's openly gay and flamboyant; the jokes are about his shamelessness and his recruiting, never about him being gay (writing style guide).
- **Where Grub finds him (v3.2):** in **his flower bed** at the bottom of the Rift (v3.4; was his plush collection), "waiting for someone to fall for him." v3.5: he is **awake**, admiring his flowers, when Grub lands on top of him and knocks them both out (the opening scene, R1). He wakes up off-screen, takes it personally and waits for Grub further in (R2).
- **The petal trail (v3.5):** Floofy sheds pink petals wherever he goes. A trail of them is his signature: R1a–R1b lead to R2, and in later areas a trail tells the player he passed through (or is waiting at the end of it).
- **Soft outside, thorns inside (v3.2):** all flowers, pastel and zest, but in a fight he is **pure thorns**: vines, spikes, nothing gentle. Hitting him does nothing: the fursuit padding is too soft to hurt (§19.0).

### 8.5 The Mirror (Furry route trigger)

  ```
  * (You've been fighting this the whole way down.)
  * (...You don't have to.)
  ```
- **Choice:** `Accept` / `Refuse`.
  - **Refuse:** Duct Tape cutscene ripping the fur off (`* RIIIIIP.` `* You are a gamer. You are a GAMER.`), FUR drops to **60**. It can happen again; the 2nd time the reflection says `* (...Again?)`; the 3rd, `* (I'll be here.)`.

- **Locks:** never on the Toxic route (FUR caps at 99: `* Your vitriol burns the fluff right off.`).
- **On purpose:** players who want the Furry route can equip the `Fursuit Head` (Furiell's house), accept hugs, sleep in Furiell's bed and wear cute armor.

## 11. Structure and pacing

### 11.1 Principles
1. One room = one idea (a puzzle, a big joke or an encounter).
2. No mandatory backtracking.
3. Random encounters on a budget: **2–3 per area** outside the Toxic route.
4. Bosses last 2–3 minutes (4–6 turns with the right ACT).
5. Puzzles solvable in < 45 s, with a funny hint.
6. Cutscenes run at most ~6 text boxes before handing control back. Floofy's R2 introduction (§19.0) is the one exception: it's longer, so its boxes are broken up with choices, movement and the fake tutorial.

### 11.2 Gamer route (main)

| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 0:00–2:30 | Front-end | Disclaimer, intro, title, instructions, name |
| 2:30–11:00 | Ruins | The fall, Floofy, Furiell, tutorials, Barky, the house, Furiell's fight |
| 11:00–17:30 | Snowdinha | Senpai Sans, Papyrus's puzzles, the town, LAN House (layer 4), Papyrus's fight |

## 23. Game overs: WELCOME HOME and GAMER OVER

### 23.1 WELCOME HOME (Gamer and Toxic routes)
- **Trigger:** HP 0 (except against Papyrus).
- **Sequence:** the soul **doesn't break**: it grows little ears and a tail, inflates in a "pomf" of pink pom-poms and gets tucked into a little blanket. Then the **`WELCOME HOME`** logo, laid out like "GAME OVER", in big soft rounded letters (Furiell's handwriting). The love-bombing won, for now. (v3: replaces FURRY OVER.)
- **Toxic corruption** (by kill count, §8.9): the logo glitches from the first kills; from the 16th kill it is replaced by **`YOU DIED`** (red serif, Dark Souls); from the 22nd, by **`WASTED`** (GTA). The blanket is gone.
- **Voice:** King Asgrr's (1:1), with the player's name:
  - `Don't lose hope, [NAME]... The ears suit you, honestly.`
  - `[NAME]! Stay determined... and maybe a little fluffy.`
  - `This is not the end, [NAME]. It is a... rebrand.`
  - `Get up, [NAME]. Your squad is counting on you. ...Probably.`
  - `You cannot give up just yet... You have not even picked a species.`
  - `Your SOUL said no, [NAME]. ...This time.`
- **Variations:** against Floofy (R2, v3.2): `Floofy? ...Oh dear. Stay determined, [NAME]. And stay away from his petals.` · against Barky: `...welcome to the pack? no? ok.` · 3+ WELCOME HOMEs (`meta.ever_transformed ≥ 3`): `...Again? Keep the tail, [NAME].` · Toxic route: no voice.
- **On continue:** back to the save; FUR returns to the saved value.

### 23.2 GAMER OVER (Furry route)
- **Trigger:** FLUFF 0.
- **Sequence:** the plush soul **loses its fur** all at once, turns back into the original red soul and **cracks** (Undertale's **original** game over, 1:1 — the joke is that on this route it's the worst fate). Then: **`GAMER OVER`**.

## 25. Visual bible

### 25.1 Global rules
- Virtual canvas **640×480**; native art at **320×240**, drawn at **2×** (Undertale's standard).
- **Colorful overworld** with a limited palette per area; **battles in white on black**, color only for special moments.
- **Nothing visually explicit** (§2.2). The ceiling is: **mosaic censor blocks**, closed objects (tab, folder, curtain), stink clouds, and the narrator refusing to describe.
- **Every character has adult proportions.** No childlike designs.
- **Readable silhouette:** every character has one element that reads in solid black (Grub's blanket hood with the headset bumps, Barky's ears, Sans's body pillow, Papyrus's cardboard ears, Sharkdyne's harpoon, Asgrr's lanyards, Mettapaw's ring light, Floofy's flower crown and rose, the Moderator's chair-mech).
- **The stench is visible:** wavy green (cartoon) lines coming off fursuits, Bottle Guy, the Moderator and the Barrier.
- **"Fluff" particles** in the air in every area (2–4 per screen, `#FFB3E6` and `#FFD27F`), denser the higher the FUR.
- **All text inside art is English** (banners, signs, screens, posters).

### 30.4 Floor messages (Dark Souls, v3)
The six gamers who fell before Grub left **messages on the floor** in the Dark Souls format (`[X] ahead`, `try [X]`, `[X], therefore [Y]`, `but hole`), each one signed with the old gamertag and a rating. **How they read (v3.5):** the normal dialog box, but with its own reveal instead of the letter-by-letter typewriter: the message **fades in slowly** with an amber/orange glow, over an ethereal, metallic chime, like Dark Souls and Elden Ring messages.
